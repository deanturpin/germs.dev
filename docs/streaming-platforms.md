# Streaming music platforms compared

!!! tip "TL;DR"

    - **Best value for money:** Apple Music — hi-res audio at the lowest major
      price.
    - **Best per-stream payout:** Qobuz, then Tidal — but check where your
      listening actually lands.
    - **Best for supporting artists directly:** Bandcamp, every day and
      especially on Bandcamp Fridays.
    - **Widest catalogue and discovery:** Spotify and YouTube, with significant
      ethical caveats.

A look at how the main music platforms stack up on price, what they pay
artists, and the ethical questions worth thinking about before you subscribe.

Prices and payout figures below are indicative (late 2025 / 2026) and vary by
territory, plan and subscriber mix. Per-stream rates in particular are averages
— nobody pays a fixed rate, and premium streams pay two to three times more than
ad-supported ones.

## The headline: streaming barely pays artists

The uncomfortable truth is that streaming is a volume business that pays
fractions of a penny per play. An independent artist typically needs hundreds of
thousands of streams to earn what a few dozen direct sales would bring in. If you
want to support musicians directly, buying music (especially on Bandcamp) does
far more than adding them to a playlist.

| ---------- | ---------------- | ------------------------ | --------------- | ------- |

| [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com) | ~$10.99 / £10.99 | ~$0.007–0.01 | Lossless + Dolby Atmos included | Cheapest major service; hi-res at no extra cost |

## Buy, don't just stream

These platforms sell music (and often physical media) rather than renting access,
so a much larger share reaches the artist.

| Platform | Model | Artist share | Notes |
| ---------- | ------- | -------------- | ------- |
| [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com) | Direct sales + streaming | ~82–85% (after fees) | 15% cut on digital (10% over $5k); pays out daily; [Bandcamp Fridays](https://bandcamp.com) waive the fee entirely |
| [Beatport](https://www.beatport.com) | Downloads (DJ/electronic) | Varies by deal | The store of choice for club and electronic tracks |
| [Discogs](https://www.discogs.com) | Marketplace (mostly physical/second-hand) | Seller-set | Definitive discography database and vinyl marketplace |

## Discovery and metadata

Not stores, but essential for finding music and cross-referencing releases.

- [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org) — open, community-maintained music
  metadata database
- [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com) — de facto largest catalogue of any
  platform, if you can tolerate the ads

## Adjacent services

Tools that sit alongside the platforms — for identifying a track you've heard,
generating "listen on any platform" links, or moving your library between
services.

### Song identification

- [Shazam](https://www.shazam.com) — the best-known audio fingerprinting app
  (owned by Apple); taps straight into Apple Music
- [SoundHound](https://www.soundhound.com) — rival identifier that can also
  recognise humming or singing, not just the original recording

### Universal links and library transfer

- [IDapp / ID.net](https://idapp.io) — generates a single "smart link" that
  offers a track on every platform at once (the source of this page's link list)
- [Songlink / Odesli](https://odesli.co) — the widely used alternative for
  universal, platform-agnostic share links
- [Soundiiz](https://soundiiz.com) — transfer playlists and libraries between
  services (handy when leaving one platform for another)
- [TuneMyMusic](https://www.tunemymusic.com) — another well-established
  playlist-transfer tool

## The ethical dimension

Price and sound quality are the easy comparisons. The harder questions are about
where your subscription money ends up.

### Artist pay

The per-stream table above tells one story, but it isn't the whole picture.
Tidal and Qobuz pay several times more per stream than Spotify, yet Spotify's
enormous subscriber base often generates more *total* revenue for a given artist —
because that's simply where the listeners are. The rate that matters is the one
weighted by where your audience actually is.

### The case against Spotify

Spotify became the industry's ethical flashpoint through 2025. The criticisms,
each with a documented source, stack up:

1. **Military AI.** CEO Daniel Ek led a ~€600m ($700m) funding round via his firm
   Prima Materia into [Helsing](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/spotify-ceo-daniel-ek-leads-690m-funding-round-for-ai-drone-manufacturer-helsing/),
   a German defence company building AI drones and weapons software. Artists
   including King Gizzard & the Lizard Wizard, Massive Attack, Deerhoof and Xiu
   Xiu pulled their catalogues, citing the "moral and ethical burden" of
   royalties funding lethal technology.
2. **ICE recruitment ads.** Spotify ran US Immigration and Customs Enforcement
   recruitment ads and declined to remove them, prompting the
   [NYC Comptroller to formally question the company](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/reports/letter-to-spotify-ceo-daniel-ek-re-u-s-customs-and-immigration-enforcement-advertisements/)
   and a "Cancel Spotify" campaign.
3. **Training AI on your music.** Updated
   [terms of use](https://musictechpolicy.com/2025/09/02/ai-implications-of-spotifys-updated-terms-of-use-your-data-is-their-new-oil/)
   were read as permitting Spotify to use uploaded music and content to train
   internal AI.
4. **Editorial playlist skew.** Major labels own a stake in Spotify and dominate
   editorial playlist placement out of proportion to their share of the catalogue
   ([analysis](https://www.music-tomorrow.com/blog/is-spotify-editorial-playlist-landscape-fair-to-emerging-artists)),
   disadvantaging emerging artists.
5. **Low royalties + demonetisation.** One of the lowest per-stream rates of any
   platform, and a
   [2024 royalty change](https://artists.spotify.com/en/blog/modernizing-our-royalty-system)
   that stops paying tracks under 1,000 annual streams at all.
6. **"Discovery Mode" as modern payola.** Artists can trade a ~30% royalty cut
   for algorithmic promotion. A 2025 class action
   ([Capolongo v. Spotify](https://www.billboard.com/pro/spotify-lawsuit-discovery-mode-modern-payola/))
   called it "modern payola"; Spotify denies the characterisation and the case
   was later pushed to arbitration.
7. **Ghost / stock music.** Reporting alleges Spotify seeds cheap library music
   into playlists to
   [dilute the royalty pool](https://harpers.org/archive/2025/01/the-ghosts-in-the-machine-liz-pelly-spotify-musicians/).
8. **AI "slop".** The platform actively surfaces AI-generated tracks, adding to
   the pool of music competing for the same royalties.
9. **Quality and price.** Audio quality lags the hi-res alternatives, and prices
   have risen across multiple regions with further US/Canada increases expected.

Several of these are contested by Spotify, and some (the payola suit) remain
unresolved — but taken together they're why a growing number of listeners are
looking elsewhere.

### Ethical and equitable alternatives

If the above puts you off, there's a well-established set of more artist-friendly
options:

- **Streaming:** [Qobuz](https://www.qobuz.com), [Tidal](https://listen.tidal.com),
  [Deezer](https://www.deezer.com) and [Coda Music](https://soundiiz.com/blog/what-is-coda-music-and-can-it-change-the-musical-landscape/)
  (an ethics-first newcomer)
- **Buying music:** [Bandcamp](https://bandcamp.com) and
  [Subvert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subvert_(music_marketplace)), a
  member-owned co-operative alternative to Bandcamp
- **Moving your library:** [Soundiiz](https://soundiiz.com) to migrate playlists
  off Spotify
- **Audiobooks:** [Libro.fm](https://libro.fm), which shares profit with your
  local independent bookshop

No single platform satisfies every value system. Deezer, for instance, is the
weakest of the streaming options on some measures, but earns its place on
robust AI policies: it tags AI-generated music, has applied for AI-detection
patents, excludes fully AI-generated tracks from recommendations, editorial
playlists and royalties, and has a global statement against unlicensed use of
copyrighted work to train AI.

### AI-generated "slop" and voice cloning

Every major platform now faces a flood of AI-generated tracks and unauthorised
voice clones that dilute royalty pools and impersonate real artists. In late 2025
Spotify introduced AI-disclosure rules, a spam filter and impersonation
protections in response. It's an industry-wide problem rather than one
platform's, but worth watching as it develops. See also [AI music](ai_music.md).

### Business model

There's a fundamental difference between *renting* access (all the streaming
services) and *buying* music (Bandcamp, Beatport, Discogs). Streaming is
convenient and cheap per-listen, but ownership and direct purchase put far more
money in artists' pockets and don't disappear when you cancel a subscription.
